Behind the Matchmaking Curtain: How Skill Ratings Decide Who You Play Against

A documented Monte Carlo simulation of the Elo rating model, seeded to match facts Lichess publishes about its own rating pool (ratings start at 1500; median stays near 1500 — lichess.org/page/rating-systems). Population spread (SD≈300) is a disclosed modeling assumption, not an official Lichess figure. Seed is fixed so results are reproducible.

Verification & assumptions

Equation: E_A = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_B−R_A)/400)); update: R_A' = R_A + K(S_A − E_A). Numerical check: R_A=1500, R_B=1600 → E_A = 1/(1+10^(100/400)) = 0.360 (confirm against View 2's slider at those values). Boundary case: R_A = R_B → E_A = 0.5 exactly (set both View 2 sliders equal to confirm). K-tiers: 40 for players under 30 games (provisional), 20 standard, 10 for rating ≥ 2000 — matching common real-rating-system practice. Limitation: this models the classical Elo formula; Lichess's live system is Glicko-2, which also tracks rating uncertainty, not simulated here.
